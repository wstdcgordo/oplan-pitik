# Pitik
***Own your data. Streamline government. Local-first, Cloud-never.***

[Introduction/Story](#introductionstory) | [Docs](docs/documentation.md) | [Technicals](docs/technicals.md) | [Benchmark/Testing](docs/benchmark.md) | [Ways to Support](#ways-to-support)

Pitik is a high-performance, air-gapped record digitization engine designed primarily for Philippine local government units (LGUs). It automates the transition from paper-based bureaucracy to secure digital databases without the costs, risks, and latency of cloud dependency.

## Introduction/Story
Pitik is born from frustration, just like how some people want to develop applications from an experience or out of curiosity, but this frustration is not just isolated to one person or a few, it's shared: long lines in some city/municipal agencies just to approve the clearance as requirement for a business permit, back and forth to municipal halls just to follow up on overlooked requirements, encoding duration, and surface-level improvement in digitization efforts.

### Why not an app?
There's a reason for that.

A local government unit (LGU) of where I live currently, consisting of almost 80 thousand residents, launched an 'application' for people to report complaints like price differences in markets than suggested ones, overpriced tricycle fares, garbage, and more; however, it's only accessible by scanning a QR code, and requires multiple steps, because it's an APK.

APKs are notorious for being breeding grounds for malware, especially relevant now that the world we are living in witnesses demonstration of power through digital capabilities. In terms of functionality, it severely limits its reach; it will never reach a sole iPhone user, and it can reach those with phones without QR code scanning function, but that takes another step, it's a tax on them. It will be very fine if the dedicated QR code directs into a webform, but it does not.

The repository was used instead of an app due to these exact reasons; the former experiences were cobra effects, in which a solution to the problem did not address it at all. This repository was meant for government use, with minimal yet detailed instructions to assuringly guide workers who would employ.

In other areas like urban cities, there are portals for users to just relay their records in time of the likes of registrations, business renewals, and etc, however, if the encoding in the city hall is still manual, it just, in a way, defeats the purpose of being _seamless_.

The repository does not just collect the records from the moment this went live, it also does collect the records way before this implementation, and this makes the digitization closer to its objectives and goals without compromising who will own the data and where it will be stored.
